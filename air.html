Teaching air to think

Pneumatic logic uses air (not electricity) to perform computations. It's an old idea that largely died out when electronic computers became ubiquitous, but we believe that pneumatic logic has compelling advantages over electronic logic in certain applications.

In 2006, we showed that our air-powered microfluidic monolithic membrane valves can control air flow (and therefore a valve can control other valves). This means that these valves can function like transistors in complex valve-based pneumatic logic circuits. Since then, we've used monolithic membrane valve-based pneumatic logic to make:

  • Latching microfluidic valves that remember their open or closed state
  • Air-powered Boolean logic gates like AND, OR, NOT, NAND, and NOR
  • An eight-bit ripple-carry adder capable of adding any numbers from 0 to 255 using only air
  • A pneumatic random-access memory (RAM) for reducing the amount of expensive hardware needed for controlling soft robots
  • A pneumatic parity bit calculator for detecting errors and faults in air-powered systems
  • A low-cost air-powered laboratory rocker suitable for use in hospitals and clinics in resource-limited settings

and more on the way.

Pneumatic logic can have advantages of electronic logic in some scenarios. For example:

  • Air-powered logic can't get hot or create sparks like electronic logic can. This makes pneumatic logic safer for use in potentially flammable or explosive environments.
  • Air-powered logic isn't damaged by exposure to water. So pneumatic logic circuits can operate safely in places like a refrigerator or an incubator where condensation or humidity might ruin electronic circuits.
  • Air-powered logic can't shock or electrocute you. That makes it safer to use in close proximity to the human body in wearables or prosthetics, or in hospitals where risks of electric shocks must be minimized.
  • Air-powered logic isn't affected by electromagnetic fields. This makes pneumatic logic circuits suitable for use in places like MRI machines.
  • Air-powered logic isn't damaged by radiation. In contract, electronic circuits on satellites have been rendered inoperable by cosmic rays and other hazards.
  • Air-powered logic can reduce the cost of pneumatic control hardware. For example, a pneumatic multiplexer can significantly reduce the number of electromechanical solenoid valves required to operate a soft robot or other pneumatic system.
