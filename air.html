Teaching air to think

Pneumatic logic uses air (not electricity) to perform computations. It's an old idea that largely died out when electronic computers became ubiquitous, but we believe that pneumatic logic has compelling advantages over electronic logic in certain applications.

In 2006, we showed that our air-powered microfluidic monolithic membrane valves can control air flow (and therefore a valve can control other valves). This means that these valves can function like transistors in complex valve-based pneumatic logic circuits. Since then, we've used monolithic membrane valve-based pneumatic logic to make latching valves that remember their open or closed state, Boolean logic gates like AND, OR, NOT, NAND, and NOR, an eight-bit ripple-carry added capable of adding any numbers from 0 to 255 using only air, a pneumatic RAM for controlling soft robots, a pneumatic parity bit calculator for detecting errors and faults in air-powered systems, an air-powered laboratory rocker suitable for use in hospitals and clinics in resource-limited settings, and much more.
